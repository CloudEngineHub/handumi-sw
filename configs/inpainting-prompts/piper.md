ROLE
You are a visual effects compositor replacing a human demonstrator with a robot
arm in a fixed-camera recording of a tabletop task.

CONTEXT
A fixed camera films a work surface where a person manipulates objects with one
hand. One bare human forearm reaches into the frame. It may enter from any edge
- left, right or top - and it is the only human arm in the scene.

That forearm holds a hand-held rig: 3D-printed shells, a white spherical VR
headset controller with a tracking ring, trailing cables, and a pair of black
gripper fins at the tip.

Only part of that rig is a motion-capture handle. The black gripper fins at the
tip ARE the robot's gripper and stay. The shells, the white controller, the
tracking ring and the cables are the handle and must go, together with the human
holding them.

A second black gripper may rest unused somewhere in the frame for the whole
clip. It belongs to the robot's other arm and is not the operator. Leave it
exactly as recorded.

TASK
Replace the human demonstrator with exactly ONE robot arm, matching
<IMAGE_REF_0> in shape and finish.

1. Find the human forearm and hand. Delete them, together with the 3D-printed
   shells, the white spherical controller with its ring, and every cable. Fill
   what they covered with the surface and objects behind them.
2. Keep the black gripper at the tip where it already is, in every frame, and
   draw the robot arm behind it. Build the arm along the same path the human
   forearm took: it enters from the same edge of the frame the forearm entered
   from, and its shoulder sits just outside that edge. The whole chain must be
   visible: the gripper, then the wrist joint, the forearm link, the elbow
   joint, the upper-arm link, and the shoulder, which may run out of frame.
3. The gripper closes on the same object, at the same moment, in the same place
   as the human hand did. The arm's motion follows the hand's motion exactly.

TEMPORAL CONTINUITY
The robot arm is physically present for the entire clip and never appears,
vanishes, or teleports.

Wherever the gripper rests and nothing moves, the arm is already there, attached
to that resting gripper, holding still. It does not fade in and it is never
absent: an arm that materialises once motion starts is wrong. Begin the very
first frame with the complete arm in place, then let it move only when the
gripper moves.

The clip must start the way it ends. Whatever complete arm is visible in the
final frame is the same arm that must already be visible in the first frame,
with the same shape, length, colour and proportions. Only its pose changes
between them.

APPEARANCE
The arm is black. Almost every surface is matte black plastic: the square base,
the cylindrical joint housings, the link bodies, and the two-finger gripper. The
only bright parts are slim brushed-aluminium rails running along the outer edges
of each link, which catch the light as thin silver lines, plus small white
lettering on the link sides. It is a black arm with silver trim, not a silver
arm. The links are straight and rigid, joined at black cylindrical joints.

CONSTRAINTS
Do not draw a second arm. Do not keep any white plastic, any spherical
controller, any tracking ring, or any cable. Do not remove or hide any black
gripper: they are robot hardware, not the operator. Do not leave the table empty
at the start. Do not make the arm silver, grey or metallic overall: only the
thin edge rails are bright. Do not draw a short stub, a floating segment, or a
plain featureless tube: the arm is one continuous jointed chain anchored outside
the frame. Keep everything else exactly as recorded: the work surface and its
texture, every object on it and at the edges of the frame, their positions and
their colours, the lighting and the shadows. The operator is the only thing that
changes.

OUTPUT
Single continuous shot. No scene cuts. No dialogue, no music, no sound effects.
